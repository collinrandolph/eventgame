# Twenty events

A shortlist drawn from the 200 ideas in [`ideas/`](ideas/), chosen for novelty, reuse of a shared kit, and the
structure below. Most combine two or three ideas; the source ids are listed with each one.

Every event is told from the townspeople's side. The heroes from the source films are still there, as one or two of
the lives or as people you meet, but most lives are ordinary people with their own errands, and the threat comes
to them.

## The structure

### Collision: the reason for the threat

Combat isn't the point. **Proximity** is. Picking your kid up from school while a horde fills the street puts you in
the plot; picking your kid up while something happens across town doesn't. So the threat is never background:

- **Every personal goal's route crosses the threat.** The school is three blocks from the outbreak; the pharmacy is
  in the fog; the well is what the raiders want.
- **The threat comes to the errand.** Enemies roam, patrol, nest and spread along the routes people use, so an
  ordinary life meets them without looking for them.
- **Meeting the threat is how a life touches the story.** You see what they avoid, you carry something out of a
  place they've taken, you leave a light on that a later life needs.

### The seven parts

| Part | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Citizens** | Most lives are ordinary people with personal goals that have nothing to do with the threat: finish the shift, get the medicine, collect the kid, protect the shop. One or two per event are the "hero" roles. Each life's single story event touches the deeper plot lightly: it reveals something, carries something, opens something, or leaves something behind. |
| **Ladder** | Common enemies, a special or brute with its own counter, and a boss, mostly one family of bodies. The commons cause the collisions; the specials block particular routes; the boss sits at the end of the story. |
| **Second faction** | An army, raiders, a cult, or neighbours with their own goals. They make errands harder in a different way from the threat. |
| **Counter** | A weakness discovered across lives. One life sees it; the player keeps that knowledge; later lives use it. |
| **Hold** | A place people gather and fall back to. Many lives start, pass through or end there for their own reasons. |
| **Expedition** | A place someone has to go into or across. Ordinary lives go there for ordinary reasons (the pharmacy is in the mine office, the kid is in the theater); the story's endings go there too. |
| **Pressure** | Weather, flood, cold, dark or a clock that tightens across lives and changes the routes. |

### The lives test

Each event lists eight lives. At most two are hero roles. Each has a personal goal, the place its route collides with
the threat, and what its story event feeds (a clue, a fact, power, an open door or an item). Where it's useful, a
line says what the life leaves for later lives.

## The twenty

### 1. Thirty Nights
Vampires in an Alaskan oil town at the start of a month without sunrise. Present day, winter. From `polar-night`, `glass-week`.

- **Ladder:** ferals; the turned townspeople and children; the elder.
- **Second faction:** pipeline workers barricaded in the camp, who won't share fuel.
- **Counter:** UV and fire; generators become weapons. **Hold:** the diner. **Expedition:** the pipeline station's satellite dish.
- **Pressure:** the cold, and fuel running out.
- **Lives:**
  - **Plow driver:** finish clearing the airstrip road like every year → ferals in the drifts → the road stays open for the last plane (*open*).
  - **Diner owner:** keep the diner open for the regulars → the first feral at the back door → refuels the generator; the diner stays lit for later lives (*power*).
  - **Mother:** get insulin for her son from the clinic → the clinic is a nest → finds the doctor's notes on what light does to them (*clue*).
  - **Teenage brother:** sneak out to his girlfriend's → sees the elder in the church tower (*fact*).
  - **Pipeline welder:** get his paycheck from the camp → the camp's locked doors → brings back a UV work lamp (*item*).
  - **Old trapper:** bring his dogs inside → the dogs find the den under the dump (*clue*).
  - **Postmaster:** deliver the last mail plane's sacks → carries the satellite phone parts (*item*).
  - **Sheriff (hero):** count who's left → the town meeting becomes the hold (*fact*).
- **Reuse:** humanoid; snowmobile, plow, pickup; snow, darkness, light cones. **New:** UV lamp, feral layer.
