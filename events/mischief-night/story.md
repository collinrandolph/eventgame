# Mischief Night: a one-story world, worked by hand

A paper test of [NARRATIVE.md](../../NARRATIVE.md) on the smallest case: one storyline, 45–60 minutes, no ending
rejection. The town is Pine Hollow ([cities/mischief-night.json](../../cities/mischief-night.json)); the event is #18
in [SHORTLIST.md](../SHORTLIST.md). Nothing here is generated: it's what a template and one run through it would
look like, to find where the rules don't hold.

---

## The storyline template

**Premise.** A small northeastern town on Christmas Eve, 1980s. Sam's father bought a strange pet at Antiques and
Curios. By nightfall it has multiplied into a brood that spreads through town. Light kills them; water multiplies
them. The antiques dealer wants it back.

**Places:** Pine Hollow Savings (the vault), the Post Office, Corner Tavern, Pine Hollow Elementary (the lab),
Haldane's Department Store (**hold**), the Strand Theater (**expedition**, the ringleader's party), Antiques and
Curios, St. Anne's (the bell tower), the Pruitt house (the generator), the Kowalski house (the plow).

**Second faction.** Mr. Lasko, the antiques dealer. In a three-story world he'd lead a competing storyline. In a
one-story world he becomes one of the story's two ending branches.

**Background:** the tavern's regulars, carolers, diner customers, the choir, shoppers at Haldane's. They fill the town in Act I and take
the mass-casualty beats.

### Entry paths (any one introduces the brood)
- **A. Nora** closes out the Christmas Eve deposits and finds them in the vault. *Fact: they nest in the dark.*
- **B. Toby** finishes his route; a sprinkler at the Post Office soaks a few and they double. *Clue: water.*
- **C. Gina** closes up early as the tavern fills with them; the stage spotlight kills a gang. *Clue: light.*

All three are planned; once one succeeds the others still play, but as chapters, not introductions.

### Developments (Act II)
1. **The weakness.** Mr. Ortiz dissects one and confirms light and water. *Prepares both endings.*
2. **The flash rig.** Walt turns his camera-flash gadget into a rig that runs off mains or a generator. *Prepares
   ending A.*
3. **Power.** Someone gets Mrs. Pruitt's generator to the Strand (emergent: needs a driver or a strong back;
   candidates by weight). *Prepares ending A.*
4. **The pet.** Sam finds the original pet. His chapter ends on a choice in the world: keep it (it hates the
   ringleader and will lead him to it) or hand it back to Mr. Lasko at the shop. *Picks the ending branch.*

**If Sam dies before choosing**, the branch is whichever ending is prepared: the rig and generator for A, Lasko alive
with the pet in reach for B. Neither means no campaign and no resolution.

### Off-screen beats
- After Act I: the diner's customers flee to Haldane's. Some of the background die on the way.
- After any death at the tavern: the regulars barricade, then scatter. Background only.
- After the generator chapter, if the driver dies on the way: the generator is where the vehicle stopped. The beat
  can't move it or close development 3; another emergent chapter can still finish it.

## What the example showed

All of the proposals below are now agreed and written into [NARRATIVE.md](../../NARRATIVE.md).

1. **It's over the target.** Ten chapters, against 6–8. A one-story world's Act III needs at least 3 chapters (one
   per checkpoint, unless a life does two), so Acts I and II get about 5 between them. The fix is three entry paths
   that are *alternatives*: play one or two, not all three. **Proposed:** a one-story world plays 2 + 3 + 3.
2. **Without rejection, the ending choice moves into the world.** Sam's keep-or-return is a decision in a chapter,
   not a menu, and it worked. But it puts the whole ending on one life, so it needs the fallback (whichever ending is
   prepared). **Proposed:** one-story worlds choose their branch in an authored decision chapter, with that fallback.
3. **Player knowledge weakens "warn someone" objectives.** When Mr. Ortiz died, "warn the store" had nobody to
   transfer to, and didn't need anyone: the player already knew. Objectives should change the world (bar the door,
   move the generator), not just pass on information. **Proposed** as an authoring rule.
4. **Moment secrets work** (since replaced: secrets now stay open across acts, with different hints in each). The
   bell tower made exploration urgent in Act II, and the bells were a cue in the world,
   not the UI. The rule that holds: cues are allowed, reminders aren't.
5. **The soft-sim rule held.** The tavern losses and the diner flight were background only; nothing closed.
6. **Suitability needs rules for children.** Sam was in the endgame as a helper, not one of the three eligible. A
   child shouldn't be picked to throw the switch, but should be able to carry the pet. Suitability needs roles inside
   a checkpoint, not just yes or no.
7. **A second-faction leader is a named character who's never played.** Lasko needed a name and a fate for ending
   B, but no chapters. That's a third kind of character between named and background. **Proposed:** "named NPC".
8. **In a one-story world, the second faction becomes an ending branch.** In a three-story world, Lasko would be a
   competing storyline. The template should say both, so the same event works at both sizes.
